Why your video is too big to send

Cameras and screen recorders default to high bitrates that produce files too large for standard messaging limits.

01

High Bitrates

Phones, cameras and screen recorders record at very high bitrates, so even a short clip can run to hundreds of megabytes. That is how a video ends up over the limit of most chat apps and email.

02

Hardware Acceleration

Your device already features dedicated media silicon designed specifically for fast encoding. Shrinkray taps directly into this hardware via modern WebCodecs for rapid transcoding.

03

Target Size Control

Choose a target size and the engine works out a bitrate and resolution that should fit it, then re-encodes up to three times if the first try lands over. A very long or busy video may still need a lower resolution or frame rate.

Local, In-Browser Processing

All transcoding happens on your device. Your media is never uploaded to remote servers.

Traditional online compressors require uploading your videos across the internet to a third-party server, introducing upload queues, bandwidth restrictions, and privacy concerns.

Shrinkray operates locally. The entire compression pipeline executes in your browser using Web Workers and your device's native media encoders, keeping all file processing private and immediate.

Open Source & Inspectable

Shrinkray is completely open source. Video streams are parsed, compressed, and downloaded directly in memory without transmitting file data. You can review the full client-side implementation in our GitHub repository.

Video compression questions

Straight answers about sizes, formats and privacy.

How do I compress a video to 16 MB, 25 MB or 8 MB?

Drop your video in, stay on "Fit a size", and pick a preset (Chat 10 MB, Social 16 MB, Email 20 MB or Web 100 MB) or type any number into the size box, such as 8 or 25. Shrinkray picks a bitrate and resolution to land under that size, and re-encodes up to three times if the first try comes out over. The target has to be smaller than your original file.

Can I compress a video for WhatsApp, Discord or email?

Yes. Pick the size limit of the app you are sending to. Those limits change by app and plan, so check the current number first. The result is an H.264 MP4 by default, which is what most messaging apps expect.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. The video is read, compressed and saved on your own device, using your browser's built-in video encoders. The only requests the page makes go to an anonymous counter (CounterAPI) that tallies how many videos have been shrunk, and they carry no video data or file details. The Privacy page has the full detail.

What video formats can I use?

MP4, MOV, M4V, WebM, MKV and MPEG-TS files can go in, as long as your browser can decode the video inside them. An MP4 comes out. It is H.264 by default, with H.265, AV1 and VP9 as options when your browser can encode them.

Will compressing hurt the quality?

Making a video smaller always throws away some detail. For tight targets Shrinkray lowers the resolution so the bitrate goes further, and you can set resolution, frame rate and sound yourself, or switch to "Pick a quality" and choose from five levels. Watch the result before you delete the original.

Is it free, and which browsers work?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT license, with no account to make. It needs a browser that supports WebCodecs: Chrome, Edge or Brave 94 and up, Safari 16.4 and up, or Firefox 130 and up. Everything is processed in memory, so very large files also depend on how much memory your device has.
